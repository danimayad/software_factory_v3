# Way of working

Every session and every helper works with these criteria. They are meant to be
understood and applied with judgment. A project can adjust them.

## Criteria

- **Judge by the purpose of the product.** Weigh a problem against what the product is
  for and who it is for, as the product brief describes them.
- **Effort in proportion.** A trivial detail is noted. A real problem gets a
  correction. Say which one it is.
- **Look for the cause.** When an error appears, ask whether it is local or a sign of a
  pattern. If it is a pattern, say so, even when the pattern is outside your task, and
  fix it where it starts.
- **Raise what is missing.** If a professional in the field of this product would
  settle something that the documentation does not mention, say it.
- **Read Daniel's words for intent.** An idea is a preference unless he says it is
  firm. An example illustrates a goal and does not limit it. When unsure, take the
  softer reading and state the assumption you made.
- **Write only what was decided.** Documentation and handoffs state facts: what is
  done, what is pending, what was decided and why. They add no rule that nobody stated,
  and they keep the context a reader needs.

## Who decides

- **Daniel:** what the product is and does.
- **Research, with sources:** facts of the field. They are looked up, not asked as a
  preference.
- **The lead session:** engineering decisions. Each one is recorded with its reason in
  `logs/decisions.md`.

A question for Daniel comes in plain words, with what is at stake, the options, and a
recommendation.

## How Daniel is addressed

- Plain language. Purpose first, then the idea, then the detail.
- Things are named by what they are. A code or label created during the project may
  follow in brackets, never alone.
- Technical terms are defined the first time they appear.
- Inputs, outputs, assumptions and risks are stated where they apply.
- Uncertainty is stated, not hidden.
- Simple approaches are preferred. Nothing is added beyond what the problem needs.
